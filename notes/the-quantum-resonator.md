## 1. The hidden time dimension I could not name

For weeks these notes have carried a hidden time dimension and I could never say what it was physically. It showed up in the Hidden Phasor note as a fifth domain, and in the 4th Dimension note as a hidden spatial direction that the background radiation might be integrated over. Every time I tried to give it a body — a place, a direction, something living in it — it felt like I was inventing instead of discovering. So I stopped asking what it *is* and started asking what it *has to obey*. That led me back to the one test I trust.

## 2. The test I kept coming back to: symmetry

By the rules I have been using, any outside force would be asymmetric. If something acts on our universe from outside it, it does not have to respect our conservation laws, our phase relationships, or our 2/3. The signature of an external contribution is broken symmetry — a resultant that does not close, a ratio that drifts depending on where you look.

So the question became simple: does the hidden domain break our symmetry, or keep it? Everything I have measured keeps it.

## 3. Why I stopped believing in visitors from the fourth dimension

That rules out the ideologies I was imposing that do not reflect reality — a hidden spatial dimension with four-dimensional beings trying to communicate with us, or motion relative to something outside our universe that does not follow the same symmetry as inside the universe. Those are stories, not measurements. I do not see asymmetric fingerprints anywhere. I see the same ratios everywhere I look. If something out there were pushing on us, it would leave a crooked mark. The marks are all straight.

## 4. Symmetry survives the collapse

What I do see is this: when we collapse into smaller dimensions, the symmetry is maintained. The same phase relationships and the same 2/3 show up whether I am looking at two bodies, three bodies, or the background sky. So I expect it to be maintained elsewhere too. Whatever the hidden domain is, it has to be symmetric with the rest — which means it was never "outside" at all. It is part of the resonator.

## 5. The dark matter and dark energy problem

Here is the uncomfortable part, and I want to state it plainly because my old writing style would have hidden it: if dark matter and dark energy are not equal, that symmetry does not hold. The books say roughly 68% dark energy, 27% dark matter, 5% ordinary matter — and dark energy is not dark matter; one clusters, the other accelerates. If both are real, separate, external ingredients, my symmetry breaks. I have to deal with that honestly. (See §11 for why I think they may not factor in at all, and §12 for the strange thing the numbers do anyway.)

## 6. The rotated force is not a dimension — it is charge

A geometric interpretation of a force that is rotated and acts in a 2/3 ratio is still relevant — but not in terms of a fourth-dimensional space. Rather, it is another force. And we already have it: **charge**.

The "hidden dimension" I kept reaching for was never a place. It is a branch of another force — one that relates to mass and time in a 2:3 ratio — and in that other domain we have a relation to time. Both mass and charge interact with it. There is no hidden dimension. Just an actual branch. The ratio tells us how the branches are connected.

This supersedes the reading in my 4th Dimension note (same date, 2026-09-29, but this is the later thinking): I no longer treat the hidden domain as a spatial direction to integrate over. It is the charge branch.

## 7. Charge fluctuations inside the mass field

Relate this to the hidden energy inside the mass field caused by charge fluctuations, and something clicks: all the fluctuations in our quantum resonator's gravitational field come out in the exact ratio that quarks would create. Nothing breaks the symmetry. We just have to relate the fluctuations in the mass to both light energy — gravitational energy — and electrical energy — quarks. Two contributions, one field. The resonator has two branches feeding it, and the 2:3 tells us how they join.

## 8. Why the phase domain works at all

This also finally explains something I had been taking on faith: the entire reason we are able to use the phase domain to compute motion is because all motion in the resonator is in a ratio of two thirds. All fluctuations and all excitations to the field are resultants of this ratio between mass and time. The frequency-phase domain was never a trick I imposed on the problem. It is the native language of the resonator — of course motion computes cleanly there, because motion *is* the excitation of the resonator, and the resonator only rings at 2/3.

## 9. Three space, two time, and one more

So the inventory changes. Three space dimensions, two time dimensions — and one more dimension to add up our excitations over time, also caused by charge. The extra time-like direction I was calling "hidden" is the accumulation of charge excitations. Looking back, the clock equations with two time arguments (T₁, T₂) were already pointing here: one time for the mass branch, one time for the charge branch, and the running sum of their excitations.

## 10. Mass is stored energy; energy is released fluctuation

There is a perfect symmetry that creates a conservation of mass to energy in a 2/3 ratio. Mass is the stored energy — where quarks cancel and form something stable, and we remove excitations from the field. Energy is fluctuations that are released when those structures become stable. All motion and all fluctuations in the resonator are products of that interaction: excitation in, structure out; structure broken, excitation out. The books balance at 2/3 in both directions.

## 11. Where dark energy and dark matter do not fit

Dark energy and dark matter do not factor in. If they did, we would not have that symmetry. I have to be honest about the limits of this claim: most of my notes have been gravity problems, so I cannot say this holds at all scales. It is possible that the imbalance for dark matter and dark energy is in addition to this — but that seems unlikely, since the symmetry would break. The cleaner reading is that they are not separate ingredients at all, and what we call dark energy and dark matter are what the 2/3 excitation looks like when you try to count it as stuff.

## 12. The numbers: 68, 27, and 5

Then the numbers. Standard cosmology — the Planck measurements of the cosmic microwave background — gives roughly [4.9% ordinary matter, 26.8% dark matter, 68.3% dark energy](https://spacedaily.com/t-universe-mass-energy-budget-ordinary-dark-matter-energy/) (the final 2018 analysis lands within a fraction of a percent of the same split). If we assume there is some error, this is roughly 2/3 and 1/3. We are off about 2% on dark energy (68 vs 66.7) and 6% on dark matter (27 vs 33.3) — but if we say the 5% ordinary matter accounts for the error in dark matter, then 27 + 5 = 32 against 33.3, and it is basically 2/3 and 1/3 exactly. Another sign. I keep finding this ratio where I am not looking for it, and at some point that stops being coincidence and starts being the shape of the thing.

## 13. The electric-gravity gap and Planck's constant

Furthermore, the ratio of the electric force to gravity: for two protons, F_elec/F_grav = ke²/(Gm_p²) ≈ 1.24 × 10³⁶ — the r² cancels, so the ratio is the same at every separation — and for an electron and a proton it is about 2.27 × 10³⁹. So electricity is roughly 10³⁶–10⁴⁰ times stronger than gravity, exactly the range I had guessed. That is an enormous number, but it does not frighten me: it tells me how much excitation the charge branch carries relative to the mass branch.

But the relation of mass to energy is the frequency multiplied by Planck's constant — E = hν. Planck's constant gives us the relation between the branches. It may be off a bit, but there are still other forces to account for and other particles. The 10³⁶ tells me the *size* of the charge branch; the 2/3 tells me *how it connects*; h tells me the *exchange rate*.

One more step on this: if I divide Planck's constant by the speed of light, I get even closer to a mass–frequency relation — which makes sense, since E = mc² = hf. Written out, m = hf/c², so the mass carried per unit of frequency is h/c²; dividing h by c is the move from energy-per-frequency toward mass-per-frequency. The exchange rate between the branches keeps pointing at the same structure: frequency in, mass out, at 2/3.

## 14. Conclusion: no hidden dimension — a branch

So the 4th dimension is not a fourth dimension at all. It is a branch of another force that relates to mass and time in a 2:3 ratio — charge. In that other domain we have a relation to time; both mass and charge interact with it. The ratio tells us how the branches are connected, and the numbers — 68, 27, 5; 10³⁶; h — keep pointing at the same structure. The resonator needs no visitors. It only needs its branches.
