## 1. The balanced case: 180 degrees out of phase

If energy and matter were perfectly balanced, then the energy we see would be perpendicular to mass — 180 degrees out of phase by the symmetry rules. Every excitation accounted for, every fluctuation answered by a mass. In that case all the energy comes from our own universe, and the books close.

## 2. Something outside is acting

But from what we hear, there is mass and energy unaccounted for — the stuff that causes the expansion of the universe, the entropy change. So we know something outside is acting. And what do we do when that happens? We check to see if it's perpendicular. If it is, then our 4D eigenstate is within our own universe: the anomaly is ours, just rotated.

## 3. Count the mass, count the energy

So we have to count the mass in our universe — and the energy from the background radiation. See if mass is energy: does the counted mass account for the counted energy? Then we measure the fluctuations to know the entropy. Entropy tells us whether it's coming from outside the universe. Which we know it is.

## 4. Which part of the fluctuation is external

The next part: even if there is more energy outside than inside, we have to differentiate between the energy fluctuations from our universe and some outside higher-dimensional source — find the difference in energy, so we know which part of the fluctuations are external. Not easy to do, since we'd have to know every mass in the universe to filter frequencies out of the cosmic background radiation.

So instead we can look at the ratio of energy to mass. If there is more energy than mass, that tells us the fluctuations are out of phase with the mass — because they are being acted on by something external. If that external energy is 90 degrees out of phase, that tells us the force generating it is 90 degrees out of phase with the motion of our universe — our 3D space's velocity.

Although we can't know the phase, for the same reason: we would need to know all the masses to distinguish which frequencies to filter and measure the phases of. We also can't perceive our own relative motion in a higher dimension. So all we can say is there is another force adding energy. We can't determine its direction.

- If it were perpendicular to our direction of travel, that would likely suggest repeating structure — our universe orbiting something.
- If it isn't, that suggests a chaotic 4th dimension, or a chaotic force.

## 5. The iterative search

What we can say is: there is dark energy coming in from somewhere, causing things to expand and entropy to increase. That likely indicates motion of our universe. We know the resultant force acting on us — but we can't separate the background radiation to get just the part that is external. Unless we can:

If we know the total mass, and we know the rate of expansion — the increase in entropy — and we know the rate of entropy, it tells us how much external energy we are looking for: a quantity. Then we convolve different signal combinations with the frequency space, and find one that results in the expected mass. If we find one that matches closely, we improve it; if we find one that doesn't, we discard it and try another combination.

Although infinitely many combinations could produce the result — so that would be difficult.

## 6. Structured versus chaotic: separating noise from signal

However, if we look at the local fluctuation as being more likely from masses nearby in our universe — chaotic and high entropy — and fluctuations that are structured as coming from an external source, we get a sense of the chaotic mass-related fluctuations in our local area as results of fluctuations in mass, of moving masses in our universe. We invert that and find the internal mass signal of our universe, and separate it: background noise from our universe, and a separate signal separated out.

Unless all the fluctuations are random and chaotic. We might find a different result: local neighbourhood fluctuations structured, and the chaotic fluctuations from outside excitations. Or if both are structured, or both are chaotic, it might be harder to tell them apart.

We should investigate techniques for separating noise from a signal, and the limits of that: matched filtering against the known mass distribution, blind source separation, hunting for non-Gaussian residuals — and where each method breaks down when the "noise" and the "signal" share the same spectrum.

## 7. Dark matter is not dark energy: the asymmetry

What we know is there is dark energy and dark matter. Therefore there is an external force. We know that dark matter and dark energy are not equal — so the resultant force's eigenstate is not acting in our universe. It comes from some external point at an angle that is not perpendicular, thus creating asymmetry.

## 8. Projecting the external resultant

If we could detect the directionality of fluctuations in the 4th dimension, we could know which direction. But we can't observe this other dimension. If mass and energy are outside, we can't represent the resultant as a force acting in our universe, but rather one acting outside it, in a higher-dimensional space.

We can project that into our 3D space by collapsing onto the plane and compressing it in one time dimension: like unfolding our background radiation sphere and letting it evolve in time. Then we would have a compressed and stretched 3D space — which is what we see with relative motion, that our space is being stretched and compressed. So we could visualize that rotated eigenplane by changing the stretching and compression into the eigen universe state: not quite our universe, but some universe in a 4-dimensional space that is squished and compressed based on the direction we rotate in the 4th dimension.

## 9. Observers at the resultant

Observers at the resultant would be included in the eigen 4D state. They would see us from some angle, but see us at every moment of space and time. They could manipulate every point in time by adding more energy into our universe from theirs.

## 10. Non-perpendicular motion and the broken symmetry

We can say: likely we are moving relative to this other 4D space, and not perpendicular. That itself indicates that the symmetry is broken — that is an indicator. Light and energy always act perpendicular. So this could be an indicator of someone trying to get our attention from the 4th dimension — or it can be just that our universe's relative motion to the one the external force comes from is not perpendicular.

The symmetry of $T^2/a^3$ that we see could then be a result of geometry: the ratio of rotation between our 3D space and the one the 4D external force comes from. A ratio of two to three would suggest the force is coming from a specific angle, and that would tell us geometry. But we must keep in mind the force in question is a resultant. We must also keep in mind that we can't detect whether the velocity of our universe is 0 or constant — there is no distinction. But we can tell there is a force in that direction, because of the dark energy. And we can suspect that it kind of breaks the usual symmetry we expect in our universe — which is that velocity and force are perpendicular. So our universe may be moving, but not perpendicular to the force. That might be the reason for the symmetry of our universe: this resultant force always produces fluctuations that follow this 2:3 proportion of frequency to mass excitation.

## 11. The quark alternative

The other thing to consider: it may not be a 4th spatial dimension at all, but another force from particles in our universe. This 2:3 ratio then stops being a geometrical one, and tells us that some other particle interacts with light energy also, and can release energy — and it does so at a ratio of 2:3.

This 2:3 seems to relate to quarks themselves inside protons, because they essentially come in pairs of up and down in groups of three. So these added excitations to the field should be structured. But the problem is all the radiation in the universe is also excitations in this ratio, so there's no way to distinguish them from the chaotic motion of masses fluctuating the field — because even the chaos follows a 2:3 ratio.

The key insight in that case would be that the fluctuations are caused by paired excitations — all of which are likely perpendicular — however they cancel in such a way that rotates them 2/3 or 3/2 out of phase. For something like quarks: quarks creating a perpendicular force at the same magnitude, since they are equivalent particles — but due to the antisymmetry, the one wave pushes the opposite way. The released energy would mean the resultant wave is 2/3 out of phase.

## 12. Conclusion: trapped light

Of the two readings — a geometrical 2:3 from a rotated 4D source, or a particle 2:3 from quarks — the quark reading is the better one. Quarks are trapped light energy: paired perpendicular excitations, antisymmetric, canceling into a resultant 2/3 out of phase. That is the signature the universe keeps writing — in $T^2/a^3$, in the background radiation, in the proton itself. The ratio was never about an angle we can't see. It was about the smallest locked system we can see: three quarks, two and one, holding light still.
